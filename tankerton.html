The Brief

From island to open house — a home that works for everyone

Marine Parade faces the cliff-top lawns of Tankerton Slopes just outside Whitstable. The view stretches across the shingle beach to the Kentish Flats wind farm, six miles out to sea. It was this outlook — and the prospect of a different kind of life — that drew Mel and Steve Payne to the area in 2015.

When the detached 1970s house came to market, sold by the family of its original owners, the Paynes saw an opportunity. Their priority was a home that could accommodate the full range of their family's needs: generous, sociable spaces for when friends came round, and quieter retreats for when they were needed. The house had to work hard.

They turned to Sonya Flynn and Mark Baker of local architecture practice Meme, who had listened carefully to how the family wanted to live. The first scheme they presented was, as Mel recalls, essentially the building they now inhabit.

"We were looking for something that was a bit life-changing. This house takes us off that island."
Mel Payne, Client

The commission presented a particular planning and structural challenge: how to completely reimagine a 1970s shell while respecting the roofline and character of Marine Parade — a street with its own established identity overlooking a protected coastal landscape.

Interior — bespoke joinery and open-plan living spaces designed around family life

The Delivery

A complete remodel — dark and stealth-like outside, full of light within

The house was comprehensively remodelled with extensions to both the front and rear. The exterior was clad in dark render, with black timber fins at ground-floor level and expansive glazing and balconies above — maximising the sea and sky views while presenting a composed, considered face to the street.

The original roof pitch was retained, with the loft space opened up and two glass triangles inserted into the gable — a detail that brings light deep into the plan. The floorplan was reorganised around a generous 6-metre opening from kitchen-dining to the south-facing garden, with a series of more intimate spaces — a ground-floor playroom, a family snug — woven into the layout alongside.

Throughout, bespoke brass-trimmed cabinetry and carefully considered colour — Mel made every choice herself — created an interior that is warm, personal, and entirely of a piece with the building's character.

The project was future-proofed from the outset. A ground-floor guest room and snug have been designed to become a self-contained front suite for Gus, should he choose to remain at home as an adult. A garden studio offers further possibilities for independence.

Crucially, the entire supply chain was local. The architects, the joiners, the bespoke kitchen maker, and the principal contractor were all from the Whitstable area — a decision that, as Mel notes, brought a level of personal care and attention that exceeded expectations.

"Nothing was a bind and I never felt stressed: I just loved every part of the process. And now we get to live in it."
Mel Payne, Client
